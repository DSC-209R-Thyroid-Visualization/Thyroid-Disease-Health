Chasing the Butterfly

One patient's path through Graves' treatment, draw by draw

Methimazole Methimazole is an antithyroid intervention I have been on for over two years. Other possible interventions include radioactive iodine, or a thyroidectomy for surgical removal.
Why "the butterfly"? The thyroid has two lobes, one on each side of the windpipe, joined by a thin bridge called the isthmus. Spread across the front of the neck, they look like a butterfly's wings. It weighs only about 20 grams, yet its hormones set the pace of nearly every cell in the body. Too high, or too low? Graves' disease makes the thyroid overactive (hyper); Hashimoto's makes it underactive (hypo). Either way, staying out of range for long takes a toll: too high strains the heart and bones, too low brings fatigue, weight gain and high cholesterol. Treatment is a balancing act, and too much methimazole can tip Graves' into hypo.

In 2017 I began experiencing heart palpitations on a regular basis. My doctor at the time had me checked out for heart-related issues, which all came back negative…

Believing that I was just experiencing anxiety, I continued with heart palpitations for another 7 years…

After I began losing weight and experiencing an increase in palpitations and hair loss, I decided to see a new primary care physician, who decided to test me for thyroid-related conditions, given that my mom has Hashimoto's disease…

I came back as positive for hyperthyroidism, and my antibodies returned positive for Graves' disease.

Now, under specialized care, I have been on the road to recovery and to better understand my body and how interventions are affecting me.

This is my data and this is my journey.
